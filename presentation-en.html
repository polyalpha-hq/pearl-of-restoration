The Problem

Processing partners are sitting on
a liability that grows every year

A high-volume depleted agricultural byproduct accumulates downstream of large-scale food processing. Marketable sugars have already been extracted; the residual carbohydrate-rich mass has no commercial pathway.

  • 250,000 tonnes per year accumulate across our initial target regions alone
  • Volumes grow every season in step with primary production output
  • The waste stream is almost never utilised; it sits in open storage and decomposes uncontrollably
  • Decomposition products contaminate soil, groundwater, surface water, and air
  • Documented regulatory precedent — multi-million-dollar fines have already been levied against operators that failed to control disposal
"Operators have repeatedly raised this issue with sector regulators and policy bodies — without a workable disposal pathway being offered in return."
Disposal fee processors will pay
$32.6 / tonne

Processors today carry the full cost of storage, regulatory risk, and eventual remediation. We replace that cost with a positive revenue stream — they pay us a fixed per-tonne fee to remove the waste; we pay them nothing for the feedstock we then convert.

Applications

Four industrial markets
address the full output

The physico-chemical profile of our activated carbon makes it directly applicable across every major sorbent end-market — from municipal water treatment through pharmaceutical purification.

Water & wastewater treatment

Effective removal of heavy metals (lead, cadmium, mercury), organic contaminants, chlororganic compounds, and pesticides from industrial and drinking water streams. Used at metallurgical plants, electroplating facilities, and municipal treatment works.

Chemicals & petrochemicals

Sorption of toxic substances, removal of volatile organic compounds from process gas streams, equipment protection against corrosion. Deployed in acid/base production, organic-solvent manufacturing, and refinery operations.

Food industry

VOC removal, decolourisation of syrups, polishing of food-grade additives. Drinking-water finishing, non-alcoholic and alcoholic beverage production lines.

Pharma & cosmetics

Purification of active pharmaceutical ingredients, removal of impurities from finished drug substances, production of medical enterosorbents. Used in deep-cleansing cosmetic formulations.

The diversity of end-markets is itself a risk-reduction story — sorbent output never depends on a single buyer industry; a contraction in one vertical is absorbed by demand in another.

Competitive Edge

Where we win
against incumbent sorbents

Versus the two dominant commercial carbon feedstocks — anthracite coal and coconut shell — our product offers structural advantages on four independent vectors.

01

Higher selectivity

The pore architecture of our material shows elevated selectivity toward heavy metals and complex organic contaminants, enabling deeper polishing than coal-based or shell-based equivalents at equal mass loading.

02

Faster adsorption kinetics

The fine-pore structure delivers substantially faster contaminant uptake — shorter contact times in the column, higher overall throughput of any treatment system using our sorbent.

03

Lower environmental footprint

By using a residual biomass stream rather than mined coal or coconut shell, we materially reduce the embedded carbon footprint and avoid consumption of non-renewable raw materials — a procurement requirement for ESG-bound buyers.

04

Structurally lower cost

Negative feedstock cost plus on-site processing (zero inbound logistics) lets us underprice incumbent producers while preserving a 62.8% net margin — competitors cannot easily replicate either input cost or location.

Production Technology

A three-stage thermal process
with controlled-atmosphere activation

The line is sequential, fully automated, and built around standard industrial unit operations. Patent protection is in place via WIPO. Every stage is instrumented; quality parameters from slide 5 trace back to specific process set-points.

Stage 01
Preparation
Feedstock pre-cleaning and concentration to remove unwanted impurities and achieve optimal viscosity. Critical for forming a uniform downstream pore structure.
→
Stage 02
Pyrolysis (carbonisation)
Thermal treatment at 500–800 °C in an oxygen-free atmosphere. The organic phase decomposes and the carbon skeleton is formed.
→
Stage 03
Activation
Steam or chemical-reagent treatment at 800–1,000 °C. Opens the pore structure, develops micropores and mesopores — the source of the material's adsorptive capacity.

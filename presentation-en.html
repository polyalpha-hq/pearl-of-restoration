The Problem

Processing partners are sitting on
a liability that grows every year

When sugar-bearing feedstock passes through membrane filtration, a depleted agricultural byproduct remains — all the marketable sugars are gone; the residual carbohydrate-rich mass has nowhere to go.

  • 250,000 tonnes per year accumulate across our initial target regions alone
  • Volumes grow every season in step with primary production output
  • The waste stream is almost never utilised; it sits in open storage and decomposes uncontrollably
  • Decomposition products contaminate soil, groundwater, surface water, and air
  • Documented regulatory precedent — multi-million-dollar fines have already been levied against operators that failed to control disposal
"Operators have repeatedly raised this issue with sector regulators and policy bodies — without a workable disposal pathway being offered in return."
Disposal fee processors will pay
$32.6 / tonne
Conservative figure used throughout this model

Processors today carry the full cost of storage, regulatory risk, and eventual remediation. We replace that cost with a positive revenue stream — they pay us a fixed per-tonne fee to remove the waste; we pay them nothing for the feedstock we then convert.

Technology

A three-stage thermal process
converts feedstock into sorbent

The line is sequential, fully automated, and built around standard industrial unit operations. Patent protection is in place via WIPO.

Stage 01
Dehydration
Feedstock is heated to 150–200 °C to remove residual water and prepare the intermediate for pyrolysis.
→
Stage 02
Carbonisation
Pyrolysis at 500–700 °C in an inert atmosphere breaks down the organic phase and forms the carbon skeleton.
→
Stage 03
Activation
Steam or CO₂ treatment at 800–1,000 °C opens the pore structure and develops specific surface area.
Primary output
Activated carbon
Granular and powdered grades
High-margin extension
Carbon-composite fibre
Optional downstream product, higher unit value

What makes the project unusual

  • Negative feedstock cost — processing partners pay $32.6 / t to dispose of their byproduct; the raw material arrives funded.
  • Zero inbound logistics — the line sits on partner-owned land.
  • Import substitution — target regional markets are presently dependent on overseas supply for ~73% of activated carbon demand.
  • Demand tailwind — market is growing at 5.6% CAGR with credible headroom for the volume to double by 2030.
  • Validated technology — WIPO-patented process, pilot sample independently lab-verified, three-stage industrial unit operations.
  • Replicable — three operating lines on identical capex and identical operating model; no bespoke engineering between sites.
